Investigation 1 — Player Position and Shooting Angle

In this investigation I keep the forward distance the same and only change the sideways position. This helps me see how position affects angle.

Real-world reference — centered shooting position

I took this photo from a centered position. It helps show why a centered shot can be modeled with a symmetric triangle from the ball to the two goalposts.

Visual 1 — Same distance, different sideways position

Visual 2 — One centered triangle

Rule for a centered player

θ = 2 tan−1((w / 2) / d)

What the symbols mean:

  • θ = total shooting angle
  • w = width of the goal
  • w / 2 = half the goal width
  • d = forward distance from player to goal line

Why the rule works: the center line splits the big triangle into two equal right triangles. I first find half the angle, then multiply by 2.

Worked example

θ = 2 tan−1(3.66 / 15) ≈ 27.4°
1
Use the real goal width: 7.32 m, so half is 3.66 m.
2
Use the player distance d = 15 m.
3
Find the half-angle with inverse tangent, then multiply by 2.

Full calculation work

Here I keep the forward distance 15 m and only change the sideways position. The goalposts stay at L = (−3.66, 0) and R = (3.66, 0).

A — Centered player at (0, −15)

Because the player is centered, I can use the simple rule:
θ = 2 tan−1(3.66 / 15)
3.66 / 15 = 0.244
tan−1(0.244) ≈ 13.7°
θ ≈ 2 × 13.7° = 27.4°

B — Wide player at (6, −15)

Distance to left post = √((−3.66 − 6)² + (0 + 15)²)
= √((−9.66)² + 15²) = √(93.32 + 225) = 17.84 m
Distance to right post = √((3.66 − 6)² + 15²)
= √((−2.34)² + 225) = 15.18 m
Use cosine rule with goal width 7.32 m:
cos θ = (17.84² + 15.18² − 7.32²) / (2 · 17.84 · 15.18)
θ ≈ 23.9°

C — Very wide player at (12, −15)

Distance to left post = √((−3.66 − 12)² + 15²) = 21.68 m
Distance to right post = √((3.66 − 12)² + 15²) = 17.16 m
Again use cosine rule:
cos θ = (21.68² + 17.16² − 7.32²) / (2 · 21.68 · 17.16)
θ ≈ 17.2°

What these numbers show

Center: 27.4°
6 m wide: 23.9°
12 m wide: 17.2°

The farther I move sideways, the smaller the visible goal angle becomes. So even at the same forward distance, position really matters.
